Our Story

A boutique built around fewer, better decisions

How We Started

It began with one rack and a lot of returns

BoutonFlareX opened on Mercer Street after years of watching the same pattern play out: a garment looked right in a photo, arrived looking like something else, and was back in a box within a week. We wanted a smaller, more honest alternative — a place where the person who chose a piece for the rail could also tell you exactly how it was made.

That meant working directly with a handful of workshops instead of a long supply chain, keeping the collection small enough that every piece gets tried on by staff before it's offered to a client, and treating the return of a garment as a design failure to fix rather than a cost to absorb.

Who's Behind It

A small team that still packs your order

We're a team of nine: two buyers, three tailoring staff, two stylists, and two people who handle everything from packing boxes to answering the phone. There's no separate "customer service department" — if you call about a hem, you're usually talking to the person who can actually fix it.

We meet every Monday to walk through what sold, what came back, and why, which is part of how the collection stays small: we'd rather carry forty pieces we can vouch for than four hundred we can't.

Our Philosophy

Quiet clothes, worn on purpose

We don't chase every micro-trend that moves through a feed. Instead we ask three questions before anything joins the collection: will this still look considered in three years, can the fabric take a real repair, and would we be comfortable explaining exactly how and where it was made? If the answer to any of those is no, it doesn't make the cut.

That's a slower way to build a boutique, but it's the only way we've found to make clothes people actually keep.

What We Stand Behind

Three commitments that don't change with the season

01

Repair over replace

A dedicated repair bench means a loose button or a popped seam is a five-minute fix, not a reason to buy a replacement.

02

Named workshops

Every product page names the specific workshop that finished it, because we think you should be able to ask us who made your clothes.

03

Honest sizing advice

If a piece runs small, we'll tell you before you order it, not after you've paid for return shipping twice.
